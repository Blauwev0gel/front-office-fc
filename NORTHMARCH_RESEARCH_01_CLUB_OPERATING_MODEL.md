# NORTHMARCH — Research 01: Club operating model & organisational evolution

Status: completed desk-research block v1, 28 November 2026. Research, not canon. Target: prototype/de-spelmaker.html, baseline dbabc15. Master Bible §10 (especially MB-44–47) remains binding. Read alongside NORTHMARCH_REAL_WORLD_FOOTBALL_OPERATING_MODEL.md (R01–R12, G01–G14); this extends organisational research rather than replacing it.

## 1. Executive findings
1. Separate functions, people, departments and employment arrangements. A role can exist without a full-time employee or standalone department.
2. Professionalisation is multidimensional: competence, routines, delegation and continuity matter as much as headcount. It can create bureaucracy and volunteer conflict.
3. Capacity is a scarce resource independent of cash. Members can contribute expertise; volunteer availability is uncertain.
4. Organisational growth should respond to workload, strategic ambition, recurring revenue and licence requirements, not automatic division-based upgrades.
5. The chairman's work evolves from assembling capability to setting strategy, scrutinising executives and managing exceptions. Operational football remains delegated.
6. Four existing department tiles remain the initial interface. Additional functions can be represented within them until a later canon decision authorises structural changes.

## 3. Comparative cases and implications
| Case | Observed organisation | Interpretation / game implication |
|---|---|---|
| Dutch grassroots (S03) | Volunteer-heavy associations | Recruitment and retention of volunteers can be a strategic resource; no universal staffing ratio. |
| English grassroots (S01–02) | Many distinct duties even at low level | Functions are obligations, not necessarily hires; combine or delegate. |
| Norway QCP (S04) | Professional routines across 22 clubs | Training and documented processes can improve continuity without adding salaries; overhead may rise. |
| Germany/Switzerland (S05) | Mixed-sport professionalisation cases | Test paid/volunteer boundary conflicts and whether hiring actually releases capacity. |
| Nordic FC (S06) | Four administrative employees, local part-time footballers | Administrative and sporting professionalism can diverge. Financial retrenchment is a plausible trajectory. |
| FC United (S07–13, S20–21) | Hybrid volunteer/paid organisation; combined portfolios evolving into specialist roles, delegated committees | Committee formation, role splitting, general manager, explicit authority and follow-up are distinct interventions. |

| UEFA licensee (S17–19) | Formal programme and staffing criteria | Future licensing may force investment, with fictional Northmarch rules separately approved. |

## 5. Operating model and progression
Functional coverage (research proposal): football coaching and selection; recruitment/contracts; youth pathway; medical/performance; sponsorship/ticketing; facilities/match operations; finance/control; administration/welfare; membership/community. Map into current Football, Academy, Commercial, Finance tiles plus existing people and governance mechanisms; no new mandatory department.

Archetypes, not levels or mandatory progression:
- Founding: combined portfolios, part-time or volunteer help, rented pitch, limited simultaneous work.
- Established: reliable routines, recurring supporters and sponsors, first specialist or outsourced provider.
- Semipro: paid coordination, separate workstreams, contracted specialists, stronger budget control.
- Professional: dedicated leadership, staff teams, compliance, facilities and succession.
- Complex institution: executive delegation, multiple teams and specialist services, cross-functional planning.

Transitions depend on recurring work, financial security, applicant availability, facilities and obligations. Relegation, outsourcing, merging roles and shrinking payroll are valid. A club may be strong commercially and weak in youth development. No hard-coded calendar or division unlocks.

### Authority matrix proposal
| Activity | Executes | Approves / sets framework | Consulted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Selection and tactics | Coach | TD sets football framework | medical, academy |
| Recruitment | TD | chairman/board sets financial mandate | coach, finance |
| Youth development | academy head | chairman allocates structural resources | TD, coach |
| Sponsorship | commercial head | board sets ethical and financial boundaries | finance, supporters |
| Staff hiring | relevant head | chairman for leadership or above mandate | finance |
| Capital project | designated project lead | board and members where canon requires | finance, council |
| Cash forecast | finance | board agrees risk policy | all departments |
| Member mandate | members/ALV | existing canon process | chairman, board |

Do not create a parallel governance/vote engine. Real club governance differs; use MB-47's fictional members-owned model.

### Work rhythm
Weekly: football delivery, injury and availability, urgent exceptions, operational commitments.
Monthly or milestone: budget variance, project progress, staffing workload, cross-department issues.
Seasonal: budget, transfer windows, youth intake, contracts, commercial campaign, annual accounts and ALV.
Multi-year: infrastructure, succession, identity and professionalisation.
Reports on meaningful change, not mandatory messages from every role every week.

## 6. Simulation proposal
Separate state objects:
- person: skills by function, availability, motivation, employment terms, remuneration, relationships, role assignments;
- function: work demand, necessary qualifications, owner, coverage, backlog, knowledge;
- department: functions, head, priorities, budget, authority, active commitments;
- work item: need, effort, duration, dependencies, owner, cost, deadline, evidence, status;
- organisation: recurring obligations, role overlap, shared services, governance and financial commitments.

Minimal implementation may use existing staff objects and four department records rather than a new framework. Avoid double counting the same person's capacity across roles.

Candidate equations (H, units to calibrate):
- effective capacity = available hours × skill factor × reliability, summed over assigned staff; allocate shared hours once.
- backlog_next = max(0, backlog + new work − completed work).
- utilisation = planned effort / effective capacity; sustained overload leads to delay, mistakes or retention risks, not a random automatic penalty each week.
- feasible activity must satisfy remaining capacity, qualifications, cash/commitments, mandate and prerequisites.
- rank alternatives by goal fit, urgency, expected benefit, cost, risk and philosophy. Only material exceptions escalate.
- hiring adds recurring cost immediately according to contract; productive capacity ramps up. Training or documentation consume capacity now and may improve later outputs. No unlimited compounding.
- volunteer availability and retention should depend on workload and organisational context with bounded uncertainty; do not make volunteers disposable free labour.

Illustrative starting test parameters only: capacity units per week 1/2/4 for volunteer/part-time/full-time, routine demand 2, research demand 1–2. These are abstract test units, not evidence about actual hours. Use sensitivity sweeps ±50%; discard values that create compulsory weekly administration.

Autonomy loop: observe state → identify needs → evaluate at least two viable alternatives (including defer) → reserve capacity and funds → execute or request authority → record outcome → update plan/knowledge. Existing MB-47 meeting slot and research occupancy remain until approved change; implement aggregated capacity behind them initially, not extra user quotas.

## 8. Example gameplay chains
A. Founding club: commercial head has sponsor leads but matchday duties consume time. Chairman can recruit a volunteer coordinator, fund a part-time administrator or narrow the campaign. Different delays and costs; staff deliver campaign independently. Follow-up explains actual sponsor conversion and remaining workload.
B. Academy-focused club: improved intake strains coaching capacity. Academy proposes a specialist; finance highlights wages; TD raises first-team pathway issue. Joint meeting sets a plan, not individual lineup instructions. Six months later assess development and minutes.
C. Growth pressure: club may gain promotion. Finance commissions readiness review; report identifies required staff and facility gaps. Chairman can budget now or accept risk of delayed eligibility under approved fictional rules.
D. Retrenchment: sponsor expires and replacement delayed. Board freezes recruitment, combines portfolios or scales down projects. Contract obligations persist; reduced capacity changes future options. Recovery is possible.
E. Succession: key volunteer announces reduced availability. Documentation, deputy or paid support can preserve continuity without mandatory hiring.

## 11. Validation protocol
- Unit checks: shared person hours cannot be allocated twice; cash ledger reconciles; committed wages remain payable; projects release capacity on completion/cancellation; authority checks identical across auto/manual routes.
- Deterministic seeded tests: same club with and without support hire; volunteer departure; sponsor loss; promotion readiness; role conflict; capacity bottleneck; refusal and deferral.
- 10-season cheap local simulation across four contrasting strategies, paired seeds; report bankruptcies, promotion, cash, recurring payroll, backlog, volunteer turnover, projects delivered, material inbox decisions and no-action weeks. No heavy GitHub Actions without permission.
- User playtest: can initiate meaningful work with empty inbox? Can explain why department chose an action? Does hiring solve a real bottleneck? Can succeed without maximum staff? Is the club visibly different after several seasons?
- Failure criteria: optimal strategy always hire, always use volunteers, or always defer; endless repeat proposals; mandatory response every week; arbitrary staffing locks; cash exploits; hidden effects; staff replacing player agency with micromanagement.
- No numerical balance claims until tests run. Preserve old saves or explicitly migrate before release.
