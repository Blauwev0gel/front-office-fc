Play the inbox

Three playable prototypes. Each uses only things you already know from email, and makes them part of the game: what you send moves your backers, and the person you wrote to answers.

Play five emails in a row: two, three and four replies, a long email and a big decision. Try Dark and 360 px too.

1. Suggested reply, with undo

Tap a suggested reply under the email and the reply window slides up and writes it for you. Send it, and you get three seconds to undo before it lands.

2. Reply, Reply all or Forward

The three mail buttons are the decision. Reply keeps it private, Reply all makes it public with bigger effects, Forward hands it to a colleague.

3. Your keyboard writes it

Reply opens the window with a keyboard, like every phone. Tap a suggestion and you see the keys light up as your sentence types itself.

Why the last two rounds failed

Round 1: slips, ledger, signatureThe answer forms were invented: slips, stamps and ledgers do not exist in email, so the screen stopped feeling like a mailbox. Everything stood still, so a decision had no moment.
Round 2: drafts, list, suggestion barThe email part was right, but the replies were again invented structures: tabs, a list of radio buttons and a toolbar without a keyboard. Nothing moved and nothing came back, so it felt like a form.

Playtest notes, ten emails each

1. Suggested reply
  • The quickest loop: chip, read the line, send.
  • The 3 second undo is felt on every email. On big calls it adds tension; on small ones it can feel like waiting.
  • Fixed: reply banners covered the meters just as they moved. They now drop in below the meters.
2. Reply, Reply all, Forward
  • The richest choices: who reads it matters as much as what you say.
  • Recipient chips glow green or red, so you see who you will please before sending.
  • Forward is off for the board vote: only you can answer that one. Reply all brings a second reply from the chair.
3. Your keyboard
  • The most satisfying to watch: the keys light up as the line types.
  • The keyboard fills the bottom half, so the email shrinks; long emails need a scroll.
  • Fixed: on big decisions the round send button had no label. It now says Hold.
