Match day

Next match is now a moment in three screens: the preview, the match and the Gazette. Play a win, a draw and a loss, and see whether you know three of your players by the end.

The flow

  1. Next match replaces Play week. It opens the preview: the opponent, what is at stake and three stories about our players.
  2. Kick off plays the match on its own screen: the pitch, the ball, momentum, moment cards with faces and a half-time talk.
  3. Full time shows player of the match and how each story ended.
  4. The Gazette brings the result and the week together, then starts the next week.

What the CEO does

You watch and care. No line-ups, no tactics. Open emails get one line before kick-off; your staff answer them if you go ahead.

Changes to the engine

Split endWeek: the preview is built before the match, with three storylines picked and stored on the fixture.
The engine gets story weight: a story player is a little more likely to feature in a chance, never enough to change the odds of the result.
Each match returns a turning point (first goal, red card or big miss), a story outcome per storyline and the momentum per minute.
The match is a full screen with its own route, not a sheet; the ticker code is reused for the timing.
The Gazette is generated from the match and the week, and opens as the last step before the next week.
The CEO only watches. Half-time shows the coach's line, with no choice, so you never pick the team.

Playtest notes

Preview
  • Three stories read in about 20 seconds.
  • The faces make the names stick by the second match.
  • Our players lead; the danger man is a single line underneath.
Match
  • About 40 seconds at normal speed, 20 at 2x, plus a half-time pause you can skip.
  • The goal flash with the scorer's face is the moment that lands.
  • Fixed: an empty pitch felt dead. Twenty-two players now shift with the ball.
Gazette
  • One scroll covers result, ratings, quotes, table and the week.
  • The story outcomes on the full-time card close the loop before the paper.
  • Fixed: the half-time card repeated the word Half-time.
