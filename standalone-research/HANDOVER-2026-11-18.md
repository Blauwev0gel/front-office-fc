## Current problem
The game simulates a club that mostly operates without the player. It substitutes notifications, dossiers, policy toggles and isolated choices for ownership. Even the isolated loop proof produced tasks that the user judged **not appropriate for a club owner**.

Do not solve this by adding random events, weekly mandatory decisions, arbitrary resources, hidden modifiers, more text, or more dashboards.

## Current research direction
Research the real activities of an engaged owner/chairman of a small professional or semi-professional football club, and translate them into weekly gameplay. Compare:
- Football Manager: inbox, information, delegation
- No. 10: Full Confidence: decisions and stakeholder responses
- Reigns: concise choices and visible consequences
- Crusader Kings: characters, authority, relationships and emergent outcomes
- Suzerain: consequential conversations and governance

Provisional design hypothesis, **not yet proven**: inbox as entry point, people/boardroom as the source of decisions, and club history as persistent consequences.

## Non-negotiable design principle
**ACTION → CONSEQUENCE → OUTCOME → NEW ACTION.**

An owner action must immediately change an actual mandate, authority, relationship, obligation, resource commitment or opportunity. Employees then act autonomously and results emerge later. The player must be able to trace those results back to earlier decisions. The owner does not choose lineups, tactics or individual scouting operations.

## Open questions
1. Which owner-specific decisions genuinely occur week to week?
2. Which actions can the owner initiate without waiting for the inbox?
3. How can a club be autonomous without making the owner irrelevant?
4. How do we validate that a decision is not merely an operational task disguised as governance?
5. How do we create attachment to club, staff, players and long-term history?
6. Can a ten-week controlled comparison demonstrate materially different outcomes for active vs passive owners?
7. Does the prototype feel like a game to a human player, not merely pass automated simulation checks?

## Next step
**Do not build Module 5 yet.** First validate a narrow owner-specific action loop with 3–5 genuinely governance-level decisions, including realistic context, authority boundaries, immediate state changes, employee execution and later football/club outcomes. Run both deterministic comparison tests and a manual playtest. Reject the loop if the player still feels like an observer or an operational manager.
